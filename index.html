Room for each other

A little light left on.

We can be surrounded by people and still feel a world away. Sometimes a song crosses that distance. Sometimes a little love does.

When pain is changing the shape of someone’s life, we want to notice the pain before turning it into a verdict about who they are. Accountability can stay. So can the possibility of understanding, repair, forgiveness, and love.

The star in the name

Elijah Star.

This mission is still small, and there’s a lot still evolving. But I know my passion will remain strong because of the star this is all named after.

He was one of the most misunderstood people I’ve ever known, and it took his death and nine years to figure out just how misunderstood he was.

May you rest in peace, brother.

Human Maps

Three ways of looking at being a person.

Not lessons. Lanterns. Each map is a different way of noticing what people do to stay close — and what they do when closeness gets hard.

How people get caught together.

Two people reach for each other and miss. One pulls away, the other pursues. Both are protecting something. Neither can see it from inside.

A map of the small collisions: misreading, protecting, freezing, pursuing, pulling away — and the moments when someone turns back toward the other and tries again.

One reaches One withdraws Both protect Misreading grows Someone turns back

How a body reads the room before words arrive.

Long before a thought forms, the body has already answered: is this moment welcome, or is it too much? Noticing that answer is a skill, not a verdict.

A gentle map of what a body does: speeds up, goes quiet, braces, softens, settles. None of these states is a flaw. Each one is the body trying to take care of you.

Speeding up Going quiet Bracing Softening Settling

What happens after a rupture.

Every close relationship breaks somewhere. The question is not whether it breaks, but what happens next — and whether anyone turns back around.

Small acts of return: staying a moment longer, naming what happened, staying curious about what you got wrong, keeping a boundary while keeping the door open. Explanation is a lantern, not a pardon. Repair is still repair even when it takes more than one try.

The rupture The pause Naming it Curiosity The return

These maps hold curiosity, not verdicts. Understanding why someone moved the way they moved never has to mean the movement was okay.

Open air Out here the sky is larger than any one story. People pass through it together — skating, talking, standing still — and the evening holds all of them the same way.

You can put something down for a moment. The sky will remember it for you.

THE HONEY DRIP
Why the river

Fear

Khalil Gibran

It is said that before entering the sea a river trembles with fear. She looks back at the path she has traveled, from the peaks of the mountains, the long winding road crossing forests and villages.

And in front of her, she sees an ocean so vast, that to enter there seems nothing more than to disappear forever.

But there is no other way. The river can not go back. Nobody can go back. To go back is impossible in existence.

The river needs to take the risk of entering the ocean because only then will fear disappear, because that’s where the river will know it’s not about disappearing into the ocean, but of becoming the ocean.

This poem is what inspired me to make music again — 9/11/2025, nine years after Elijah died.

The river does not keep score.
